Water Damage We See Most in Akron

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Akron households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Akron. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Akron homes is well along before a stain appears. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Cincinnati Restoration Co crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.

How Drying Works on an Akron Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Akron property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

Extraction is always step one, since pumping and vacuuming remove water much faster than any drying machine. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. Most homes in Akron reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
