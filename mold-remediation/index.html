Maintenance Tips for Mold Remediation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Make sure crawl spaces are properly ventilated and consider installing a vapor barrier
  • Address condensation on windows, pipes and cold surfaces promptly
  • Use mold-resistant drywall and paint in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and basements
  • Aim for 30 to 50% indoor humidity and never let it stay above 60%, running dehumidifiers and ventilating as needed
  • Mold in ductwork is prevented by servicing the heating and cooling system and replacing its filters on schedule
  • Check that exhaust fans in the kitchen, laundry room and bathrooms move enough air

Why Mold Remediation Is Not a DIY Job

Mold remediation is not a DIY project. Disturbing mold colonies without proper containment sends millions of spores airborne, which can contaminate unaffected areas of your home and create worse health exposure than the original problem. Capturing and containing the spores during removal takes HEPA-filtered scrubbers, protective equipment for the crew and containment kept under negative air pressure, which is how professionals do it.

Professional mold remediation also addresses the root cause of the growth, which is the moisture source. Removing visible mold without correcting the underlying moisture problem means the mold will return, often within weeks. Certified remediation technicians have the training and equipment to identify hidden moisture sources, dry affected areas properly and put solutions in place that prevent recurrence.

Warning Signs for Mold Remediation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Spots you wipe away that keep reappearing in the same place
  • A slow leak under a sink, behind an appliance or in the roof that went unnoticed
  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
  • Peeling wallpaper, bubbling paint or warped materials that point to hidden moisture
  • Condensation forming regularly on windows, walls or cold surfaces
  • Fuzzy or spotted growth on stored boxes, clothing or the backs of furniture
  • Water damage not long ago that was left to dry without professional equipment
